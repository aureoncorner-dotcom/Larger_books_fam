1. What This Part Is Actually Excavating
Part I followed the geometry from balls to shaft. Part II follows what happened after the shaft acquired a boot file, a dialogue protocol, a mediator, a third key, a very stern anti-capture policy, and eventually the good sense to ask whether the mediator was a type error wearing office casual.
This is a developmental provenance record, not a victory lap and not a retrospective myth in which every early metaphor secretly knew the final mathematics. The question is narrower and harder: what appeared when; what changed; what survived; what was discarded or demoted; and which contradiction, failure mode, or evidentiary constraint forced the next architectural turn.
The result is a canonical spine plus an evidence appendix. The spine is readable. The appendix keeps the receipts, forks, acronym collisions, timestamp inversions, missing originals, and places where Git history is still required before anyone gets to use the word “definitive” with a straight face.
2. Chain of Custody, or How Not to Crown a Filename
Three link classes govern this excavation.
* DIRECT — the cited artifact or recoverable Drive revision contains the claim, wording, or architectural feature being described.
* RETROSPECTIVE — a later artifact describes an earlier state, but the earlier original has not yet been independently read.
* UNRESOLVED — ordering, authorship, exact wording, or file identity cannot yet be established from the available record.
A source may be DIRECT for what it says now and RETROSPECTIVE for when it says an older idea first appeared. A reconstruction labeled “CTA-I,” for example, is direct evidence of the reconstruction and only retrospective evidence of CTA-I. Version numbers are not timestamps. Drive creation and modification times are custody events, not guaranteed authorship dates. A quoted hash is a reported hash until the named bytes are actually supplied and checked.
This discipline matters because the archive is a living midden. Some documents are native Docs, some are Word exports, some are copied packets, some are later reconstructions, and one file currently named v5.3 contains earlier v5.1 and v5.2 revisions inside its own Drive history. The monkey did not leave a museum catalog. The monkey left strata.
